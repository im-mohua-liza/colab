Is Research at the Top of the Hierarchy?

1 December 2025

Roughly 8% of the total workforce sits within the education sector — which raises a real question: is progress in education actually driven by research, or mostly by the researchers producing it? The distinction matters more than it sounds.

How the roles actually distribute

Structures vary a lot by geography, but a recognisable pattern shows up almost everywhere: research doesn't sit neatly "above" implementation in a hierarchy — it shows up, in different forms, at every level of the system, from the people designing policy to the people running a classroom.

What actually drives a change in practice

Take a recent education reform in Delhi as an example: it wasn't the product of one study or one decision-maker. It emerged from interconnected systemic factors — academic findings, practitioner experience, political will, social trends and resource constraints all moving at once, each contributing at a different level to how the change was actually implemented.

Reframing the question

Researchers aren't at the top of a hierarchy — but research genuinely is present at every level of one. Effective programme design tends to weigh five things together: what practitioners on the ground actually need, what the academic research says, broader societal trends, the resources realistically available, and the political will to act. None of those five is sufficient by itself; the interventions that hold up are the ones that integrated all five rather than leaning on just one.
